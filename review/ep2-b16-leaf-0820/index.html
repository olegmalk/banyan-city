THE QUESTION. Beat 16's brief asks for a leaf-as-subject macro shot, which your "average leaves" ruling forbids. Restage the beat — what should the shot be instead — or licence this one shot to feature the leaf shape?

One word is a complete answer, and both chains are already written down in pipeline/decisions-pending/ep2-b16-leaf-0820/ so neither comes back to you for a second decision. "restage" (say: both leaves, the whole plant as subject, him behind it) — satisfies canon and drops the clause the sampler has now refused three times; nothing is filed from here because the new staging is yours to write. "licence" — keeps the macro and writes the exception into canon as an exception, so it cannot drift. Or name a third staging.

Read section 6 before you answer. The third path — the big-leaf composite that was named and held back — has now been built and sampled, so you can look at it. It does not come out, and that bears on the choice.

1. The two things that contradict

The brief, from the approved script — node.md beat 16, WHY:

Close on the sapling's leaf; the scavenger sits blurred behind it.

The shot list states the same relation harder: "close on one leaf with the scavenger blurred behind it; the leaf is the subject and he is depth."

The canon, from your 08-17 answer, now canon.yaml sapling-cotyledon-shape:

"the sapling 2 leaves are average leaves"
— which the entry reads as: ordinary, plain, unremarkable, the shape anyone draws when you say "leaf". Out are deeply lobed five-fingered palmate fig leaves. And out is any leaf drawn as a feature — no exaggerated silhouette, no leaf whose shape is the subject of the shot.

That second half is the one that bites. It was written to stop the wrong leaf being drawn; read plainly, it also rules out a shot whose whole job is to make a leaf the subject. Beat 16 is the only beat in the episode that asks for exactly that.

4. Why this comes to you rather than to another render

  • Wording — closed, three rungs, above.
  • The beat's done_when is a relation, not an object: "the leaf is the subject and he is depth". Beat 03 already established that a relation cannot be bought with words.
  • The composite that worked four times is not this shape. Beats 15, 19, 03 and 13 composited a small plant into a plate — 4.1 % of frame on b13 — and let a light pass make it belong. Beat 16 needs a large object drawn in front, over most of the frame; a pass over 60–80 % of a picture is a re-render, not an inpaint, and a leaf at 800 px must carry venation and line weight a 30 px one never had to.

6. The third path, built — and it does not come out

One large ordinary leaf drawn in the near foreground with image processing, then put through the same 0.30 finishing pass that made the small composited plants on beats 15, 19, 03 and 13 belong in their frames. $0, five seconds of GPU, one sample.

The two pictures are the same picture. That is the finding. On the beats where this method works, the pass turns flat shapes into proper drawn line work and you can see it happen. Here nothing happened.

The veins at 1:1, no resizing. Slightly softer on the right, and that is the whole difference.

Measured, so it is not just my eye

  • The detail in the leaf went down, not up — 10.45 to 9.41. When this method works, that number stays about the same and the detail moves into edges. Losing it is the other thing entirely.
  • It is not that the model gave up on a big area. It moved just as many pixels here, per pixel, as on the version that worked — 6.65 against 6.23 — across a mask eight times the size. It worked and it changed nothing structural. That is now the second beat today to show that the model trying hard predicts nothing about whether the picture is right.

And two things I noticed that no measurement asked about

  • It reads as a mature tropical leaf — a banana or rubber plant — rather than as a seedling's. The outline is ordinary, which is what your "average leaves" ruling governs, but the size and the vein pattern say grown plant, which is a different thing from what beat 16 is about.
  • He is not blurred behind it, he is gone. The brief asks for "the scavenger sits blurred behind it" and the shot list for "he is depth". His face is entirely hidden; only scalp and ears survive. The leaf is the subject and he is absent, which is half of what was asked.

What this means for your answer, plainly. Granting the licence would licence a shot this house cannot currently build. That is not a reason to refuse it — if the macro is what beat 16 should be, then it is worth solving and the next step is a strength rung rather than a fourth wording. But you should know it before you grant it, and it makes restage a stronger option today than it was this morning.
