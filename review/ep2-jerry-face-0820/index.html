Jerry's ears, and the tusk nobody ever drew

Two design picks on one character, surfaced by curating his LoRA training set. Nothing is blocked on either. Batch them with your next look; if you say nothing, v1 trains on the mixed set exactly as it stands and neither answer is foreclosed.

Building the 31-frame training set for Jerry meant looking at every frame of him we have side by side for the first time. Two things fell out that no ruling covers. Both are taste, so neither gets decided here.

The reason they are worth one minute of your time now rather than later: a LoRA averages whatever it is fed. Trained on the set below, it will learn "Jerry's ears are whatever, roughly" and "Jerry has no tusk" — and those become the defaults every ep3 plate inherits, quietly, without anyone choosing them.

A. The ears

The script asks for a specific thing. node.md line 35:

A SCAVENGER — goblin-ish, enormous ears, one broken tusk, patchwork cloak — sprints into frame, skids, and dives behind the sapling's thin trunk.

The identity clause you ratified on 2026-08-19 does not mention ears at all — lean wiry adult goblin man, green skin, bald head, patchwork cloak. So the prompt says nothing, and the engine improvises per frame. Across 31 frames the range runs from no visible ear whatsoever to long swept-back elf ears, and every one of those frames is a plate we kept.

"Enormous ears" was in the original brief and has simply never made it into the clause the plates are actually drawn from.

B. The broken tusk

Same line of node.md asks for one broken tusk. In 31 frames it has never once been drawn. Fangs show up — small upper canines, visible in idx 14 and 17 — but a single broken tusk, the asymmetric detail the script names, does not exist in any frame we own.

This one is not an oversight, and you have a fingerprint on it. On 2026-08-10 you called the close-ups "bad character consistency, makign the goblin look too complicated and ending up making t bad", and at ~15:45 the same afternoon you ratified the opposite recipe on beat 15 — tags only, green skin, nothing else. The fix that came out of that was ep2-b04-goblin-close-0811, one variable: tusks removed.

So the tusk was dropped to buy simplicity, and it worked. What was never asked is whether you meant to drop it permanently or just off that one over-stuffed prompt. The script still says he has one.

What I need from you — two words each, and they batch

EARS. Pick a lane and I write it into canon and into every plate prompt:

  • Enormous — honour the script. Big, prominent, unmissable. Silhouette reads goblin from across the room.
  • Long and pointed — the idx 17 look. Elegant, elf-adjacent, currently our most striking frames.
  • Small and rounded — the idx 13 look. Downplays the ears, leans human, makes him read sadder and less creaturely.
  • Leave it free — you like the variation and do not want it pinned. Legitimate answer; I stop treating it as drift.

TUSK.

  • Bring it back — one broken tusk, as written. I add it to the clause and accept it will cost some consistency, since it is exactly the kind of small asymmetric detail the engine fumbles.
  • It stays gone — and I edit node.md so the script stops describing a character we do not draw.

Or "you pick, it is not a taste question" — and I rule both, log them, and you never hear about it again.

What happens if you do not answer

Nothing stalls. v1 of the LoRA trains on the mixed set as it stands, which is the deliberate choice: it inherits the average rather than committing to a reading you have not made. Both questions stay open and cost one retrain to apply later — the retrain is ~55 GPU-minutes and $0. I would rather leave v1 honestly mixed than bake in my own taste and call it canon.
