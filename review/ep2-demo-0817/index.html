The freeze test every lane has been quoting is weaker than it reads

This page was about to print “no frozen tail” as a measured fact on two new picks. It cannot, and this is the retraction before the claim rather than after it.

The instrument behind every terminal_freeze_index figure in our record (review/ep2-picks/exact_freeze_index.py) asks one question of each adjacent pair: are these two arrays the same picture, measured as ncc == 1.0000 at four decimal places. That is an identity test. A research lane demonstrated with a synthetic control that an encoded 3×-held clip has a nonzero delta on all 71 of its consecutive pairs — it passes the identity test cleanly while visibly holding every picture for three frames. So “terminal-freeze index 0” means “no bit-identical consecutive frames were found”, and it does not exclude a held picture. Every such verdict in our record, on every beat, is softer than it has been stated.

So the real check was run — on every clip in this cut, and on a control built from our own footage at our own encoder settings. ffmpeg -vf mpdecimate counts how many frames survive near-duplicate removal; total ÷ kept is the hold factor. The control, rebuilt and re-measured from scratch for this page rather than quoted: beat 11's own pick, held at 2× and 3× and re-encoded at crf 33 like our clips, reads 1.96× and 2.74×; the same clip re-encoded without any hold reads 1.00×. So the instrument separates a held clip from a moving one on our own material, at our own encoder settings, with the hold factor tracking the real hold period.

One limit of it found here, and it cuts against the instrument rather than for it. A previous version of this page credited another lane with 2.77× on a held clip under heavy grain. That did not reproduce: the same 3×-held control with heavy synthetic grain laid over it (noise=alls=28) reads 1.52×, not 2.74×. Grain pushes a genuinely held clip toward “no hold”. So the grain figure is withdrawn from this page, and the direction of the error matters: a 1.00× reading is a filter that failed to find a hold, not proof that none is there — most safely on a noisy clip. Our clips are LTX output at crf 33 with no grain added, which is the case the control above covers.

This corrects a fault description that has been on this page since yesterday, in the direction of the footage being better than stated. Yesterday's page said beat 17 judders — 96 frames carry about 32 distinct pictures, an effective 8 fps and called that the same defect the rest of the cut has. Measured this way, beat 17 carries 93 distinct pictures of 97 (1.04×), and the rest of the cut does not share the defect: eleven of sixteen clips sit between 1.00× and 1.13×. The recorded 8-fps figures came from a lag-3 autocorrelation, and our own record already warns that that family of measure aliases (every odd hold period aliases to exactly 1.00x). Both readings stand. The same correction applies to beat 11's pick, whose record says it holds every 3 frames, ~32 distinct pictures; mpdecimate keeps all 97 of its frames, and the control above says the instrument would have caught a 3× hold if there were one.

What this instrument is NOT. It is a near-duplicate detector with a tolerance, not a motion measure. 1.00× means no consecutive near-duplicates at mpdecimate's default thresholds; it does not say the figure moved a lot, or at all. That question is still only answerable by looking, which is the next box.

And this is why these lanes read frames by hand: there is no off-the-shelf metric that can answer our actual question. A research lane confirmed that no available automatic measure distinguishes the figure moved from the drawing was re-inked. VBench's dynamic-degree measure is boolean per clip, thresholds at roughly 16.5 px on our frame size, and resamples to 8 fps — so a 24 fps 3× hold is structurally invisible to it; its four other temporal dimensions score a frozen video near 1.0. The literature's answer for animation is a benchmark plus human double-blind reading. So the frame-by-frame reading behind every verdict on this page is the correct instrument, not a stopgap standing in for one. Every number quoted here is a filter; the reading decided.

What this page does not claim

No camera-scale figure is quoted for beats 02 or 03. The lane that read those 31 clips deliberately made no scale claim, because it built no instrument with an internal control. So every word about framing on those two beats — enlarged, cropped, feet in frame, never leaves frame — describes what is in the frame and nothing else. That restraint is preserved here on purpose.

Beat 11's background-scale numbers are different, and that difference is the point. They come from a similarity transform fitted to the background only, validated on five gates — including a synthetic pull-back ramped to 0.70 that it recovered as 0.701 / 0.700 and a synthetic pan it read back as zero scale change. Two of those gates caught real bugs in the tool and both failures are left in its source comments. That is a real instrument with an internal control. Its numbers are not extended to any clip it did not measure, which is every clip on this page except beat 11's fourteen.

And a tool defect worth knowing about rather than fixing: pipeline/coldread_frames.py's motion-window rule latched onto an early micro-move on two beat-03 clips, so its dense strip did not cover the actual fold — a lane reading that strip would have called a performing clip frozen. --pad 60 forces consecutive coverage of the whole clip; a lower --threshold does not fix it.
