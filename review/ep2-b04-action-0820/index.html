2. What any replacement has to keep

  • The VO line, unchanged and unre-recorded — "Worst stealth plan I have ever seen — and I once watched an architect hide an outage in a footnote." All three options below are written to make that line land harder, not to accommodate it.
  • The beat's job — he is hiding badly, in the six seconds before the guards arrive. Beat 03 put him behind a trunk that hides roughly one-sixth of him; beat 05 brings two patrol guards in at a jog.
  • What the engine is good at — gross body motion. It draws stand-ups, ducks, leans and tilts readily; it has now twice refused to move one small feature while the rest holds. Every option below moves something big.
  • An ONGOING action rather than a static attitude — one measured finding says a terminal clause naming an ongoing action holds position without costing the performance, where a static attitude makes every frame the last frame. All three are phrased as ongoing.

3. The three

A — THE PEEK · the one I would pick, and the pick is yours

The scavenger leans out from behind the trunk to look, and pulls straight back the moment he has looked — the whole head and shoulder make the trip, twice, and he is still doing it when the shot ends.

Why this one. It turns the exact defect into the beat. Rung 1's failure was that the engine swung his head 64.7 px when it had been told not to; here that swing is the action, so the one thing the engine has already proved it will do unbidden becomes the thing being asked for. It is the only option on this page with a rendered demonstration that it works — the top row of the picture above is, accidentally, a rehearsal of it.

The joke gets better. Leaning out from cover to check whether anyone can see you is the worst possible stealth, and the line calls it that over a picture of it. R3 wants the comedy to be the mechanism; here it is.

Scorable as: his head crosses the trunk's edge and returns, at least twice, with motion in the last twenty frames.

B — HIS OWN HAND OVER HIS OWN MOUTH

The scavenger claps a hand over his own mouth to keep the held breath in, holds it there, and is losing the fight with it as the shot ends.

What it buys. An arm entering frame is large, unambiguous motion, and "held breath" is stated by the picture rather than inferred from a still face. Silencing yourself when nobody has spoken is its own joke.

The risk, named rather than buried: this asks for the object this engine most recently destroyed. Beat 09's clip, rendered today, has a hand resting at a cheek — full hand at frame 1, two fingers by frame 6, gone by frame 8. A hand at a face is exactly the shot that dissolved. It may well work here and it is a real option, but it is the only one of the three carrying a fresh same-week failure of its central object.

C — THE SLOW SINK

The scavenger holds his breath and sinks slowly lower behind the trunk as it runs out, a little smaller every second, and is still sinking when the shot ends.

What it buys. The grossest motion available and by far the cheapest to score — one scalar, the top of his head, over 121 frames, monotonic or not. No isolated feature, no hand, no head/eye coupling to fight. Of the three this is the one most likely to simply come back working.

What it costs. It is the least funny of the three and the furthest from what you originally wrote — "eyes darting" is alert and this is deflating. Hiding by becoming shorter, far too late, is a joke, but a quieter one than A's.
