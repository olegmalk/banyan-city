Beat 17 — the beat finally does what the script says

Beat 17 is GOODBYE. On 2026-08-18 its line was restaged, from brushes off to gives his cloak a shake, on the strength of eight seeds that measured 8/8 stand, 8/8 turn and 0/8 brush — the engine would not draw the brush. Nothing had been rendered against the new line, so the beat had no take matching its own canon. Three takes followed. This is the third, and it is the only one that keeps the action, the world and the garment at once.

All five clauses PASS, against a bar written before the render existed:

  • S1 stand — PASS. He comes up off the grass.
  • S2 the shake — PASS, and this is the load-bearing one. Across f030–f041 he lifts the cloak up and out into an extended sheet, sweeps it down across his body and lets it settle: one full out-and-back of the hem across roughly a third of the frame, feet planted.   • P2 the garment — PASS. Cloak hue 223.8° at sat 0.58 (f036) and 231.1° at 0.47 (f060), inside a required 180–260° at ≥0.40, against a plate of 220°/0.65.

How the previous two takes died, because it explains what this one is

Take one drew the shake on its very first seed — the engine that would not draw a brush in eight seeds drew a shake immediately — and then abandoned the plate in eight frames: 92% of all colour drift was complete by f008, a blue sky over green meadow becoming an amber sky over a dry brown field. Part of that was self-inflicted; the positive prompt said afternoon light warming toward amber.

Take two deleted those six words and nothing else. The plate held — 13.6 at f008 where take one read 80.7 — and every action clause held with it. But the cloak became a pink-and-cream poncho from f024 on, and the plate clause passed it anyway, because that clause measures whole-frame distance and a cloak is a small share of the pixels. Right idea, wrong granularity.

Take three, the one in this cut, changed one variable again — the positive prompt now names the cloak's colour — held the seed, and added the garment clause that take two's failure showed was missing. It passes all five.

The checkerboard is the plate's, not the render's

The cloak in this take is a blue-grey with a bold checkerboard weave, where the incumbent's was plain mauve. That is a visible change and it is worth saying where it came from: the pattern is already in the init plate. The plate was pulled and looked at rather than assumed — sha256 74e8eccf…, the same file the job's own step manifest names:

Named gap, not a failure: the garment clause tests hue and saturation, which is a colour test. Nothing in any bar tests the cloak's pattern, and the checkerboard reads bolder in the back half of the clip than in the plate. That is unmeasured. It is recorded here rather than scored, because inventing a clause after seeing the pixels is how bars stop meaning anything.

Two honest costs, neither of them scored

1. It is the least mobile of the three takes. Source interframe median 0.50 — the lowest of the chain. In the cut, beat 17's slot measures an adjacent-frame median of 0.40 with 58 of 112 frame pairs near-duplicate; the incumbent measured 0.31 with 72 of 112. So the beat is barely livelier overall than it was. The cloak moves more while the clip moves less, and no clause on any of the three beat-17 specs asks about overall motion. That is a shared blind spot, named rather than patched.

2. The cut rewinds its last 0.67 seconds. The clip is 4.04s and beat 17's slot is 4.71s, because the slot is sized to the VO. When footage is shorter than its slot the pipeline fills it by palindrome — the clip forward, then the clip reversed. Proven frame by frame, not assumed: cut frame 97+k is the source's frame 96−k at a residual of 3.8–4.2, identical to the 3.8–4.7 baseline of matching forward frames, while the distance from a held-last-frame hypothesis climbs monotonically to 37. The last sixteen frames are the source's f096 down to f081, played backwards.

This is the same mechanism that put the backwards tail on beat 01, and it is much quieter here: a reversed walk cycle still reads as a walk cycle, where a reversed growing fig reads as a fig un-growing. It is still a rewind. Not fixed, and for the same reason as on beat 01 — shortening the slot means cutting the VO, filling it means a longer clip, which is a render, and replacing the palindrome with a held frame is a pipeline change that would apply to every beat of every episode and should be argued before it ships.

What it does not fix

This is a different plate from the incumbent's, so the swap is a swap and not a tweak. The beat still ends on a walk-away rather than the script's afternoon light, and the guards-vs-cast consistency problems elsewhere in the episode are untouched by it.

Beat 18 — the fig trembles, and you cannot see it in a contact sheet

Beat 18 is THE DECISION: Extreme close-up: the one fig on the thinnest branch, stem trembling. The incumbent was a steward pick with no lane verdict on the clip at all. Three motion seeds had been sitting finished on the box since 2026-08-17, unjudged. All three were judged overnight against the bar their spec pre-registered.

Those eight frames look identical, and that is the point worth carrying off this page. They are sampled every 17 frames. The tremble is fine and fast; a sheet sampled at that spacing cannot see it, and neither could a sheet sampled every 15 frames see the strobe that failed one of its siblings. Both defects and both passes on this beat live between adjacent frames.

Six consecutive frames of the stem, cropped

Every second frame across a quarter-second. The stem's curve leans, straightens and leans back. That is the beat.

The comparison is a finding in its own right

Three seeds of one recipe returned 1 pass, 1 strobe, 1 decay — s20260872 pumps the specular past 25% on 28 of 120 adjacent pairs, and s20260873 moves for the first half and stops dead (quarters 9.22 / 4.10 / 0.72 / 0.45). That means the strobe is seed-borne, where the 2026-08-12 read had it looking like a property of the recipe. Opposite conclusion, and the reason three seeds were filed instead of one. It also means no fourth seed is licensed: 1-in-3 is this show's normal rate.

A correction to yesterday's page

The 2026-08-18 page said of beat 18 that “in the cut its last frame holds for 5.9 s”. That is wrong, and it was wrong about the mechanism rather than the arithmetic. There is no held-frame path for real footage in the assembler — a clip shorter than its slot is palindromed and then looped. Verified frame by frame on both cuts: in the 2026-08-18 cut, beat 18's frames 122–242 are its source's frames 120 down to 0 (residual 1.9–2.0, matching the 1.6–2.0 forward baseline), and the held-frame hypothesis is off by 10 to 24.

Flagged, not fixed: yesterday's rows for beats 04, 06, 11 and 16 use the same “last frame holds for N s” phrasing and are therefore suspect too. Only beat 06 was spot checked here — its final twenty frames are not still (jumps of 12–15 levels every third frame), so that row is wrong as well. Beats 04, 11 and 16 were not measured and are not being asserted either way. No clip changed and no verdict changed; only a description of what the assembler does with them.
