Beat 13, THE SHADE — one question, and it needs a look rather than a read

Four rungs, four failures on the same clause, and every mechanism that could fix it is now closed by measurement or contradicts canon. This page is the two frames and the one question. $0 — no render was made for it.

PRIVATE REVIEW PAGE · A TASTE CALL (R4) · NOTHING IS BLOCKED WHILE IT WAITS

THE QUESTION. Does this satisfy "tips his head sideways into the sapling's hand-sized patch of shade" — or may the plant be drawn taller than canon in this one shot so the shade can actually reach his face?

One word is a complete answer. "A" — that's the shade, ship it — and the beat closes with the clause written off; nothing is filed, because the take is already in the cut. "B" — draw it taller here — and a one-shot exception goes into canon as an exception, and the motion rung fires off it. Both chains are already written down in pipeline/decisions-pending/ep2-b13-shade-0820/, so neither answer comes back to you for a second decision. Or name a third thing — and section 5 below is my argument that you should.

READ SECTION 5 BEFORE YOU ANSWER. Since this page was first written I built option B and rendered it, so you could look at it instead of imagining it. It comes back a balloon on a wire — and the reason is not the drawing or the sampler. It is that "taller" and "exactly two leaves" are both canon, and together they cannot make a plant.

1. Why it cannot be filmed as written — the measurement, on the beat's best frame

This is ep2-b13-shadelit-0820 at its last frame, the deepest head-tilt any rung has produced. He is seated on the ground; the sapling is at his hip. His eyes are about 320 px above the top of the two leaves, and the leaves themselves are about 60 px of plant. There is no head position in this staging that puts his face inside that shadow.

Measured off the frame, not asserted. The same reading appears twice in the code that draws this plate: beat13_shade_composite.py records that a canon 40 cm plant at his depth would crown at y 404 — his brow, "not what the approved line stages", and it carries a hard guard NO_DRAW_ABOVE_Y = 640 so nothing it draws can reach his face. A chest placement was tried and rejected by eye as "a flower pinned to his poncho".

2. This is the best the beat has ever looked, and that is the point

The right-hand frame is what an exposure rung bought this morning for $0 and no new words: same prompt, same seed, same mask, same anchor — one tone curve on the plate. It repaired a defect nobody had asked it to repair, the face going illegible in the middle of the shot.

So the question is not "can this be made prettier first". It is asked on the best pixels the beat owns, which is the only honest place to ask it.

3. What was already tried, so this is not a rerun of an old ask

  • Wording — three rungs. The middle-placement edit that repaired beats 03 and 15 gave this beat its performance back and did nothing for the shade.
  • Exposure — tested and exonerated. "Beat 13's plate is the dim one" is true of the field and false of the face: his cheek reads 114.6 against beat 03's 101.8 and beat 15's 93.3. He already had the brightest face in the episode.
  • Raising the plant — refused by the plate code itself, on canon: the sapling is about 40 cm and always shorter than he is, in every beat of 002b. That refusal is exactly what this question asks you to override, or not — and it has now been overridden once, on purpose, so that you can see the result. Section 5.
  • Lowering his head further — that is a new plate with a different pose. Authored work, not a derivation, and nobody should start it before the question above is answered.
  • The instrument — the measurement that was supposed to score the shade was retracted: it passes on the clip it was written to reject. Scored by eye instead, which is another reason this comes to you rather than to a number.

4. The line it is being judged against

The scavenger's legs give out and he drops to sit in the grass at the base of the stem, then tips his head sideways into the sapling's hand-sized patch of shade — the only part of him it will cover — knees up around his ears.

Your own earlier ruling is the other half of the tension: "thats ridiculous, lmao. the sapling is tiny." The line asks for shade on his face; the ruling makes the plant too small to cast it. Both are yours, so which one gives is yours too.

5. Option B, built and rendered — and it does not work, for a reason worth your time

You should not have to rule on a description, so answer B was drawn and put through the same 0.30 finishing pass that made option A's plant belong. Same plate, same palette, same light, same recipe, same seed. One variable: the plant is 921 px instead of 485 px — about 48.7 cm against canon's 40. $0, five seconds of GPU.

Both are finished frames, not drawings — each is what the motion rung would actually be conditioned on.

It reads as a tulip. Two leaves overlapping into one heart-shaped mass on a long bare wire. Not a sapling — a lollipop.

The crown at 1:1, no resizing. Left is my flat drawing; right is after the model finished it. On option A that same pass turns flat shapes into proper cel line work. Here it barely changes anything — and when you cannot tell the drawing from the render, the render is a paste.

And it is not the sampler's fault, which is the part that matters

The obvious explanation would be that the model disengaged on a mask twice the size. It did the opposite. The pass moved more pixels here than on the version that worked — 8.62 average levels inside the mask against option A's 6.23, over 7.97 % of the frame against 4.10 %, and against sky and foliage rather than grass. The model worked harder and the picture is worse. So the recipe is not the problem.

THE ACTUAL FINDING, AND IT IS WHY I THINK THE ANSWER IS NEITHER A NOR B. A half-metre-tall plant carrying exactly two seed leaves is a lollipop by construction. Seed leaves sit near the ground; nothing alive is 50 cm of bare stem with two of them on top.

So "draw it taller in this one shot" does not override one rule — it collides with a second one, also yours: the sapling has two leaves. Height and leaf count are the same ruling seen from two sides, and you cannot lift one without the other.

Which makes the honest third option this: the shade lands when the plant has more than two leaves — when it has grown. That is a story question about where in the season beat 13 sits, not a shot question, and it is not one I can answer or should pre-empt. If that is the direction, beat 13's shade clause is not broken; it is just early.

One more constraint the build turned up, in case you want B anyway: at this framing the plant has to fit a 248 px corridor between the edge of the frame and his face — the frame edge on one side, and on the other a hard rule that nothing I draw may touch his face. Two blade angles were tried and both broke it (one ran off frame, one landed 114 px onto his cheek). Only a near-vertical pair fits, and near-vertical is why they overlap into one mass. A tall plant cannot have wide leaves in this shot.
