Before you start

The two frozen identities are provisional and yours to veto this morning. They were written by the steward under your 01:5x delegation — “clothing - whatever, guards - whatever … things that aren't in the script you can decide” — against a laptop about 35 minutes from dying, and every round on this page was rendered against them:

  • Goblin: green skin, bald head, patchwork cloak. Bald is your ruling; the cloak is the script's own clothing (beats 02 and 08 stage him “in a patchwork cloak”). Chosen off a sample where two of four frames were inspected, not four — the time box is recorded rather than smoothed over.
  • Guards: two round guard men in plain brown tunics, silly harmless bureaucrats with round soft bodies and bare heads, both faces clearly visible, one holding a bark clipboard. The tunic sheet was picked on register fit; the robe and vest sheets were never fully compared.

Needs a human call

Seven things the wave settled enough to hand over. Each is a decision, not a task — nothing below is queued, and none of it is a task list waiting on your yes.

1. A sapling next to a figure is a model limit, not a prompt bug

Beats 02, 03, 15, 17 and 19 all ask for a plant beside or in front of the character, and the checkpoint keeps refusing the composition — a bed of generic seedlings instead of one sapling, a stem parked at the frame edge, giant leaves floating with no stem under them, or a sprig in his fist instead of a plant in the ground. Two fix rounds were spent on it (beats 02 and 03) and it moved to exactly 2/4 once, on beat 02.

The decision: accept 2/4 on these beats and pick from what lands, restage the beats so the plant and the figure are never asked for in the same frame, or take the composition out of the text channel entirely — inpaint or composite the sapling, which is the route you already named for the beat-01 fig. A third prompt-level round would be re-buying an answer we have twice.

2. The pale scalp has now defeated both levers, and the definition is the remaining suspect

Both available fixes were tried and measured. The negative (no scars, no stitched skin) cleared the pale dome on beat 02's medium shot but left it untouched on beats 04, 13, 15, 17 and 20. The affirmative (his bare scalp green) was fired on beat 03 and holds the colour in 1 of its 4 frames. Beat 08 is the natural experiment nobody designed: it is the only beat whose frozen goblin definition omits patchwork cloak, and it is the only goblin beat with clean green skulls in both generations, with no ban ever sent.

The decision is yours because the definition is yours: take patchwork cloak out of the identity slot and put the cloak in the staging sentence where beats 02 and 08 already stage it, keep the definition and accept a cream crown on close-ups, or keep both and fix the crown after the fact. The definitions are frozen and the steward will not move them.

3. The guards still have no character, and five beats now show what fills the vacuum

The freeze gave the guards a costume and a body shape but no person. Beats 05, 06, 07, 09 and 10 all drew a hard, scowling, muscular adult man instead of a silly harmless bureaucrat — and beat 05 shows the wording alone does not bind, because that beat sends the bureaucrat words and still drew the muscular men. No round attempted it, deliberately, so the camera and costume results stayed attributable.

The decision: whether now is the time for the guard character you called for on 2026-08-11 — “should probably setup a character for the guards, take your time doing that” — or whether ep2 ships with whatever the checkpoint's default man looks like.

4. Armour came back on beat 05, on the one axis you ruled outright

You rejected armour outright and the first generation honoured it in every frame of every guard beat. The beat-05 fix round changed only the camera, left the armour, helmet and knight bans untouched — and s3's rear guard arrived in a riveted breastplate with pauldrons and metal bracers. So the ban is not what was keeping armour out, and one frame in the beat's fix round violates your ruling.

The decision: whether a single armoured seed voids the round for you, or whether it is a seed you simply do not pick from.

5. The second figure never arrives, and two independent fixes disagree about why

Beat 07's partner was being banned by the recipe's own negative; retiring that ban did not produce him in any of the four frames, so the contradiction was real but was not the cause. Beat 10 still puts a floating disembodied head (s1), a drawing on a clipboard (s2) and a giant pale sphere (s3) where the second guard belongs, while the same lexical fix cleared beat 09's severed heads completely. Beat 08 grew a second goblin instead.

The decision: whether these beats get restaged as single-figure shots that cut to the second person, or whether more GPU goes into asking one image for two characters. The evidence says the checkpoint is bad at the second body and there is no measured lever for it yet.

6. The beat-03 fix frames overwrote their own baseline on the results branch

The beat-03 r2 job published its stills into farm-out/ep2-b03-idfix/ — the original round's directory — so on farm-results-rtx5090 that folder now holds the fix frames, not the baseline. main still has the original. The pair on this page was built by keeping them apart by hand.

The decision, before that branch is merged: move the r2 frames into farm-out/ep2-b03-idfix-r2/ on the branch first, or merge as-is and lose the only comparison the round exists to support. Every other fix round published to its own directory, so this cannot recur from the same source.

7. Two more rounds were written for beats 04 and 14 and never rendered

After the fix generation came back, r3 predictions were staged for beats 04 and 14 proposing both levers together. No frames exist — the results branch has no r3 directory — and their stated premise is that the affirmative held the crown colour on beat 03, which this page measures at 1 of 4. On beat 14 the scalp was never pale to begin with.

The decision: whether to fire them at all. They are recorded in the ledger as unscored predictions rather than quietly dropped, and the GPU is free.
