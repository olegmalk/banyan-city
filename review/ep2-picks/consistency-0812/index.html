The short version

The guards are fixed. Two rounds of sheets drew one man where the script has two, eight frames out of eight. Round three drew one man on purpose and got a clean full-length turnaround in four of four — so the pair now comes from casting two of those four seeds as two different men, rather than from asking one picture to contain a pair.

The goblin works, and we now know which part of the picture does the work. Conditioning beat 02 on a reference gives back the creature in the reference — bald in sixteen frames of sixteen, red-eyed in all of them. A reference transfers what is inside it: head crops locked his face and let his clothes change every seed, and a crop reaching the coat hem put his own coat on him four times out of four while keeping the face readable. That crop is the reference now.

And he is the same goblin in a beat he was never tuned on. Beat 17 — nothing like beat 02 compositionally — drawn from the same reference bytes gives back the same creature in sixteen of sixteen. That is the sentence this whole page exists to be able to write.

The guards' pictures came out and then did not carry. Beat 06 drawn from the two cast guards produced neither of them, and lost their bald heads to hair in about fourteen frames of sixteen. Beside the goblin that is informative rather than merely bad, and it is written out below.

Two things need you, and neither is a picture. The guards' register, and one small change to our own code that the fair version of the guard test cannot happen without.

1 · The references — provisional

The goblin

Picked from the round-1 sheet, seed s0, on your named axes: simple, no scars, bald, one eye colour. It was chosen over cleaner-looking turnarounds for a mechanical reason — it carries three large views of one face, which is the most face there is to crop, and cropping is what conditioning eats. These four crops are what the beat below was actually conditioned on.

The guards — round 3, and the two men cast out of it

Rounds 1 and 2 are on the reference page as the control. Round 3 changed exactly one thing: it asked for ONE guard. The reason is worth a sentence, because it was not a weak prompt — reference sheet, multiple views, turnaround is the grammar for one character seen from several angles, so “two guard men” was read as one man drawn twice, and round 2 then banned different characters right next to a two-character request. The blank mannequin faces of round 2 also disappeared here, with no face tag added, which says those were eight tiny figures crowding one frame rather than a missing word.

Four seeds of a solo turnaround is a casting call, so two of them are cast: guard A is s0, guard B is s2. They carry every frozen fact at once — bare head, plain brown garment ending above the ankle over trousers and boots, a clipboard in hand, a face with features — and they are visibly two different men, A longer-faced and leaner, B rounder and softer. s1 is the best drawing in the round and the least like a clerk: a lean stern man in a mandarin-collar coat, holding nothing. s3 is the one floor-length robe, which is the drift the tunic was frozen to prevent.

The finding, and what is running because of it

A reference transfers what it contains. That one sentence explains every row above. It also says the next move is not “push harder” — a whole coat came through intact at the gentlest setting we use — but “show more of him”. So the round on the card right now holds the beat, the strength, the seeds, the prompt and the source frame absolutely still, and changes only how much of the character is inside the picture: head, head and shoulders, down to the coat hem, whole standing figure, with the original head crop copied in byte-for-byte as a control.

Two honesty notes on those crops. The pictures are padded to square before they are sent, because the code centre-crops a reference to a square and a tall whole-figure crop would otherwise arrive as a coat with no head. And the two stray heads in the corners of the source frame — the blob, and the tan-skinned one that is the only colour break in it — are painted out rather than cropped out, because a crop tight enough to lose them also loses the character's hands.

The ladder came back, and it drew a clean curve

The four control cells returned identical to the round above — same reference bytes, same frames — which is the only reason the other twelve mean anything. On that footing: head and head-and-shoulders behave exactly as before, a faithful face over clothes that change every seed. The crop reaching the coat hem puts his own toggled coat on him in four of four and keeps the face big enough to read. The whole figure adds the trousers and boots and shrinks the face. The strength knob was never touched and never needed to be.

A fault I put there, and how it is fixed. Both wide rungs drew extra small goblins in three of eight cells. That is the crop, not the model: the source frame is a sheet, so a crop wide enough to hold the coat also holds fragments of the neighbouring head views in its corners, and painted-out rectangles kept missing ear tips. The reference is now cut by connectivity — keep the one connected blob of ink containing the standing figure, remove every other mark on the page. 3 · The answer to the actual question — is he the same goblin in a different beat?

Everything above measures one beat across four seeds, and you never said the goblin changes between seeds. So: beat 17, which has nothing in common with beat 02, drawn from the same reference bytes. Sixteen of sixteen come back as the same creature — pale yellow-green, bald, long pointed ears, red eyes, his own toggled coat. The top two rows are the coat-hem crop, the bottom two the whole figure.

Two things worth pointing at. The coat-hem crop gives the bigger, brighter face; the whole figure gives the fuller outfit and a smaller face — which is why both are kept and each beat gets to say which suits its framing. And the clothes did something new: beat 17's own text asks for a patchwork cloak while the picture wears a toggled coat, and what came back is a toggled coat with patches and stitching on it. So a prompt and a reference only fight when they name incompatible things, like tusks on a tuskless face. Where they name compatible things they merge.

On that footing the remaining goblin beats are being drawn on the frozen recipe, two at a time, judged one at a time. Nothing about the recipe changes between them — same reference bytes, same settings, same seeds — so the only thing each new beat can tell us is whether he survives its framing.

4 · The guards, conditioned — and it did not carry

Sixteen frames of beat 06 drawn from the two cast guards: guard A whole figure and head in the first two rows, guard B in the last two. Neither man is in any of them, and the bald heads are gone — pale swept hair in roughly fourteen of sixteen. These are a measurement, not beat candidates; the draft they were drawn from asks for the armour you rejected.

Why this is a finding and not just a failure

The goblin's baldness held sixteen of sixteen in beat 02 with the word “bald” nowhere in that prompt — it came from the picture. Here two bald references lost to hair. Same mechanism, same strength, same settings, opposite result. What differs is what the model already believes about the noun: a goblin boy is hairless before anyone asks, a guard man is a haired soldier. At this strength a reference can confirm what the model already leans towards and cannot overturn it.

And the test was not fair to the reference, which was written down before it ran. The code sends each beat's original draft, and beat 06's asks for mismatched armour and never says bald — so the words fought the picture over the clothes and gave it no help at all on the head. The fair rerun is the same job against the tunic-and-bald wording that already exists in the drafts file. It is blocked; see below.

No conditioning knobs were turned after this result, deliberately. The rule was written before the render: if neither guard is recognisable, the fork stops and comes to you, because a knob hunt on an unfair test is how a week disappears.

5 · What is blocked, and by what

One line of our own code stands between us and the fair guard test

Earlier today no guard beat could be conditioned at all — the launcher accepted only beats carrying the goblin. That was fixed within the hour and the guard round above is the first job through the new door. What remains is smaller and more annoying: the sampler always sends a beat's original draft and has no way to ask for a different wording, so the tunic-and-bald version of beat 06 — which is already written and sitting in the drafts file — cannot reach it. Rewriting the original in place would silently change what every other job sends, so that is not a workaround, it is a trap. The change needed is a variant flag on the sampler; it is a pipeline edit and it is handed over rather than made here.

Until it exists, the honest status of the guards is: the reference sheets work, the two men are cast, and whether they can be carried into a beat is untested rather than disproved.

6 · The one thing that needs you

The register

Three rounds have asked for silly harmless bureaucrats and drawn solemn, faintly military men — 0 of 4, 0 of 4, 0 of 4. The two guards above are cast on your frozen facts, not on your tone, because the tone never arrived. If the tone is the more important half, then this casting is wrong and another sheet will not fix it: the phrase is not reaching the model at all, and the next thing to try is a different channel for it, not a fourth round of the same ask.
