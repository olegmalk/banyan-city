A correction — I claimed a defect here and I was wrong

This section said, for about two hours, that the pass's instrumentation was broken and that half its GPU time rendered duplicates. That was my error. The retraction stays on the page rather than getting quietly deleted, because the wrong version is what you would have read.

What I claimed: that ten of thirteen output directories produced byte-identical frames across different reference images, and that every sidecar recorded the same four references — so the conditioning experiments could not have varied what they claimed to vary.

What is actually true: I audited the wrong field. I read ip_adapter_reference, which stores a filename, and the pass reuses s0…s3 as slot labels — so naturally it looked constant. The field that records which image was actually used is ip_adapter_reference_sha256, and it is present in every sidecar. Read against it, the duplicate counts I found are fully explained with nothing left over: the frozen directories carry two distinct references duplicated into four slots, which is exactly why r0 equals r1 and r2 equals r3 — by construction, not by fault. Two directories carry one reference in all four slots. The directories that were supposed to differ carry four distinct references and produced sixteen distinct frames. There is no defect and no wasted GPU time, and the earlier lane's findings stand.

How I got it wrong, since that is the more useful part: I found a real pattern — the duplicate counts are correct and were worth noticing — and then reached for the most dramatic explanation instead of the cheapest one. A field that comes back constant is evidence it is not the discriminator at least as much as evidence the runs were identical, and I never checked which. Listing the sidecar's own keys would have taken three seconds and shown me the right one sitting there.

Nothing else on this page depends on that claim. The beat-by-beat scoring above was done by eye on the frames themselves and is unaffected.
