Update, one batch later: this wall has a door in it. What I originally wrote

Beat 19's colour problem is fixed — I named the light warm and gold and banned blue and teal, and both seeds came back warm. But its other problem did not budge. The prompt says "a small sapling", singular, and "one slender stem"; the negative bans row of saplings, many saplings, orchard, planted rows. It drew several anyway.

Explicit singular, explicit ban, both ignored. Colour is addressable. Number is not. That is the first genuine wall I have hit in two days of this — everything else that looked like a wall turned out to be me not saying something.

And naming a time of day is not the same as naming a colour. Beat 19 got an explicit temperature and stabilised. Beat 17 only had its contradiction removed — it now says "late afternoon" and nothing about warmth — and it still comes back grey at one seed and gold at the other. An afternoon is as free to be grey as gold. So the rule is to pin the colour itself and ban its opposites, which is the same shape as everything else that has worked here.

And one of my fixes made a beat worse

I wrote a single "healthy plant with two broad green leaves" clause and dropped it into three beats at once. On beat 3 it worked — the bare pole became a real sapling in both seeds. On beat 18 it made things worse: that beat is an extreme close-up of one fig, and I told it to draw a plant, so it drew a branch with leaves and several figs hanging off it.

That is my error and a specific one — I applied a fix by category without checking it against the beat it was going into. The retry drops the clause and tries something else: since I cannot ask for one fig, I describe a frame too tight to hold two — the fig filling the frame edge to edge, the lens too close for a branch to fit. Count enforced by geometry instead of by counting. If a second fig turns up even there, then number needs a tool rather than a prompt, and that becomes your call.

Two rules for anyone writing these prompts, learned the hard way tonight

Both came out of near-misses in the last hour, and the lane converting episode 3 should have them now rather than rediscover them tomorrow.

  1. Never edit a prompt by substring — use whole words. I stripped the word "noon" from a prompt that had three times of day in it, and turned afternoon into after. It would have rendered. I caught it by re-reading the finished prompt rather than trusting my own edit, which is the only reason it is a footnote and not a wasted round.
  2. Re-read the whole prompt after layering anything onto it. Beat 17 ended up asking for morning, afternoon and noon simultaneously because I added an environment paragraph to an older prompt and never read the result end to end. The model had been choosing a time of day for me on every run. Layering is how these prompts get built, so this will happen again to whoever builds the next one.

Both are the same failure at different scales: trusting what I meant to write instead of reading what I wrote.
