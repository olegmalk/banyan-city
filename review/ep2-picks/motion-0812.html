What I am watching for: whether the face stays the same goblin for all 145 frames. An extreme close-up gives LTX the most surface to drift on and gives you the least room to miss it. Locked-off at guidance 2.0 is the recipe with a measured result behind it. If you want the push-in it is one flag and one re-render.

A defect in the pass itself, for the record

Not something you need to decide — something the next lane needs to know before it trusts this pass's numbers.

The four rounds per beat are four different reference images. In ten of the thirteen output directories the results are byte-identical across different references: r0 equals r1 and r2 equals r3, so sixteen frames are really eight; in two directories all four rounds are one image, so sixteen are really four. Four references collapse to two outcomes, or to one. On top of that, every sidecar in all thirteen directories records the same four reference filenames at the same scale and the same window — including the directories named cropscope, charref, headref and crossbeat, which are supposed to differ in exactly that.

This does not change the verdicts above; identical images stage identically. It does mean roughly half this pass's GPU time rendered duplicates, and that any earlier conclusion phrased as 0 out of 4 at each round was reading the same file two or four times. Pipeline code is not this lane's to change, so this is reported and left.
