Beat 07 — the one that stopped being a slate

Eight frames evenly spaced across all 97. Frames 1–3: plate pose. Frame 4 on: the arm is up and aimed.

What the verdict measured, and why the arm matters more than it sounds. No wording ever aimed this point. Text-to-image put the arm on the goblin 3 of 3, and across 12 of 12 stills it could not aim a point at all. i2v did both in one seed: the guard's arm lifts at f045, the index finger lands at the goblin's throat and collar by f050, and the point holds to f096 — 47 of the 97 frames. It was measured at his own shoulder, so a camera move could not be scored as the action. F2, "the goblin points", was the pre-registered most-likely failure and never fired; nor did F1 frozen-arms, which is the exact failure that killed beat 18's crf-10 take. 0 of 96 frame pairs under 0.5 interframe.

THE FAULT THAT TRAVELS WITH IT, AND YOU CAN SEE IT IN THE STRIP ABOVE: BOTH MEN ARE WEARING THE SAME UNIFORM. The init dresses the scavenger in the guard's own pale wrap tunic and white sash, so a beat that has to read as an authority pointing at a scavenger currently reads as two officials in matching kit. The cause is this repo's own positive-placement law for the fifth time: the scavenger's noun phrase carries no garment at all, so the only clothing terms in the prompt are the guard's and they land on both men.

The plate is recorded UNAPPROVED AND PROVISIONAL by its own spec. ep2-b07-scavcostume-0819 is the rung that answers it. The earlier lane's judgement that this plate's faults block promotion is not overturned here — it is narrowed from three faults to one, and the clip is in a bench cut with the caveat attached. Nothing about beat 07 is promoted, picked or ack'd by this page.

The three slates that are left, and what each is actually waiting for

Beat 09 — THE PAUSE — the engine can act it; the plate cannot cast it yet. That is a real change of state, because until tonight nobody knew whether this engine could move a face at all. It can: holding the init at crf 10, the eyes go from downcast behind the lenses to narrowed at f021 to fully closed with the brows drawn at f121, and the hand travels off the cheek — the face working through a thought, performed by the picture rather than described by the prompt. The conditioning numbers moved the way they had to (first-step face difference 39.34 → 0.66, background drift 60.89 → 9.89: the subject moves and the world holds). What is not there is the man. The plate's hair comes back brown and shaggy where guard A's is near-black and cropped (7 of 7), the eyes open at a rate of 1 in 4 rather than on command, and the face reads adolescent — and no seed has ever produced the tight framing and open eyes at the same time. So both existing clips are barred from any cut by their own headers, and the beat is a slate on purpose. Its VO is in the cut over the card, which matters here: beat 09's line is the episode's punchline.

Beat 15 — GOOD LISTENER — unblocked twice today, still short one thing. §6 came off it (the restaged lines were read and approved) and the "we cannot get him and a rooted plant into one frame at canon scale" blocker is dead — its new plate does exactly that, twice running. What is left is the leaf count: the beat needs two cotyledons and the wording ladder is closed at two rungs with the count still wrong. The named route is a composite init, which is a build.

Beat 19 — THE DROP — it missed this train by about an hour. Its plate blocker died twice today: three draws put the fruit aloft, and the plant that three wordings could not turn into a two-leaf sapling was drawn instead. Then the first motion sample came back and failed in an instructive way — the plant grew and lay down, the fruit never detached, and the clip rendered beat 20's line ("picks the fig up with both hands") inside beat 19, with no hand action in the prompt. The failure is subject attribution, not amplitude, so a fifth wording is the wrong move and that lane says so.

Where the whole episode actually stands

Every beat's state was re-derived once today into review/ep2-picks/cut-readiness-0819.yaml — one row per beat with the take's sha256, the verdict that licenses it, whether its VO exists and what it is blocked on, each row citing the cut's own ingredient hashes, a verdict block in a job spec, or the beat's done_when. The honest headline is not the slate count.

Four beats have a written PASS against a bar that was pre-registered before the pixels existed: 01, 07, 17, 18. Thirteen are best-available. Eight of those thirteen — beats 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 13, 16 and 21 — have no verdict block of any kind, and have ridden five consecutive cuts unchanged.

They are not suspected of being bad. They have never been judged, and five appearances in a cut is not five passes. The slates are the loud, tracked problem; the quiet middle is where the unknown in this episode actually lives. It is named rather than fixed, because judging eight beats is its own unit of work with its own bars — and because inventing bars for footage that already exists is exactly how a bar gets bent to fit the clip.

What this cut cost and what it did not touch

  • $0, no GPU, no synthesis. One ffmpeg assembly, two local file copies and one scp off the box. Every clip in it was rendered by another lane and judged by another lane before this page existed.
  • The VO is byte-identical to the 2026-08-19 cut. All 18 mp3 files and all 18 json manifests hash the same — verified rather than asserted, because "same VO byte for byte" is a claim a checksum can refute.
  • Eighteen of twenty-one beats have a VO take and the other three are silent in the script (beats 2 and 21 are pure action; beat 19's text is stage direction). Nothing in this episode is waiting on voice.
  • Nothing is promoted. Bench assembly via --out: no leaf, not in lineage.yaml, no plate_ack, no pick written by this page. Every swap here is veto-able in one line, and the two clips that were replaced are still on disk in review/ep2-demo-0819/sources/.
