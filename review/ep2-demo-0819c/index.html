Beat 19 — the fall, in the cut

Eight frames across the beat's five seconds, taken from the assembled cut and not from the source. The fig hangs, falls, lands and rests. Nothing else in the frame changes — that is measured, not asserted: max difference outside the fig's path corridor is 0 in all 120 frames, and frame 0 is the picked plate byte for byte.

Why it is a composite and not a render, which is the interesting part. The render was tried first and failed decisively. Put through the proven LTX image-to-video recipe, the same plate produced this: the plant grew and lay down, the fruit never detached, a second fig appeared, and 81 of 121 frames put fruit pixels inside the body zone you ruled disqualifying. The engine rendered beat 20's line — "picks the fig up with both hands" — inside beat 19, with no hand action in the prompt and both hands on knees in the plate. Its verdict named the cause as subject attribution, and this repo now has five rungs saying another wording does not fix attribution. A sprite moved over a static plate cannot revert the plant, cannot make him reach and cannot grow a second fig, because nothing is regenerated.

Two more limits travel with it, also verbatim: "THE LAST 2.792s IS A STILL FRAME. An honest hold, not a disguised one." And "THE EVENT IS SMALL: 48px of travel on a 33px fig in a 1280-tall frame" — which is your own standing card, since knee-height canon caps the fall at about 30cm however it is produced.

Nothing about beat 19 is picked or promoted by this page. Its own record says so: "beat 19 stays a SLATE until the author says otherwise." It is in a bench cut so the question — is a 48px drop with no notice in it the beat? — can be answered on pixels instead of on a description. That question is yours.

Worth knowing before you judge it: four rounds were built and rejected before this one, and three of the four had a fully green bar — they were caught only by opening the pixels. A clone-fill that put a bright fringe down the fig's left side; an interpolation that produced an olive rectangle of bands because the pixels above-left are his cast shadow; a solve that came out dead flat; and a landing where the fig hovered over its own contact shadow because the centre was solved with a rectangle's bound instead of an ellipse's. A clause was added to the bar after the fourth so the next one cannot pass that way.

The palindrome audit — eight beats are being played forwards and then backwards

This has never been published in a cut's manifest before and it is checked here for all 21 beats. When a beat's slot outruns its clip by more than 0.05s, render_t3 plays the clip forward and then reversed, and loops that to fill the slot. It prints nothing when it does this. It is not a bug — you verified the mechanism in loop cycle 005, because a plain loop restarts as a hard jump-cut mid-shot and a palindrome keeps every seam motion-continuous. But "not a bug" is not the same as "invisible", and in this cut it fires on 8 of the 18 footage beats: 1, 3, 4, 6, 10, 11, 17 and 18.

Beat 06 across 0:29–0:35 of this cut. The board goes up, down, up. He turns the bark board over, un-turns it, and turns it over a second time.

Beat 06 is the worst and it reads as a continuity error. A 1.917s clip in a 6.71s slot, so the material runs three and a half times. The beat's verdict praises that board turn as genuinely performed — and it is. The cut shows it performed, rewound, and performed again.

Beat 01 is subtler and still there. A 5.042s clip in a 9.46s slot. Its verdict passes on "growth monotone over 85 frames, 0 shrinks" — true of the file. In the cut the second half runs backwards, so across the beat the fig goes green, ripens to purple, and un-ripens to green again. The PASS is a claim about the source; this is a claim about the cut, and they are different claims.

The other five are not worth your attention — margins of 0.13s to 0.67s at the tail on beats 3, 4, 10 and 17, and beat 18 is a tremble, which reversed is still a tremble.

The cause is not the tool: the voice is longer than the footage. Slots are voice-led by design, because playing full clips over short dialogue left multi-second dead-air tails and drew a wince in loop cycle 004. Every margin above is a beat where Chatterbox says more than 4–5s while LTX produced 4–5s of picture. The fix is longer footage or shorter lines, per beat.

And this is where beat 19 nearly died. Reversed, beat 19 is a fig flying back up onto a stem. It is 120 frames at 24fps against a 5.000s slot — margin 0.0000s — so the branch cannot be reached, and the frame count is a constant in the tool with that reasoning written beside it. The earlier attempt cleared the same branch on 0.09s of luck.

“guard 2 has the same voice as the sapling…”

Confirmed, and it is the only one. Measured on this cut's own VO takes (median F0 by autocorrelation + spectral centroid, pipeline/qa_voices.py's estimators): the sapling sits at 107.4 Hz / 1182 Hz and Guard 2 at 109.1 Hz / 1283 Hz — 1.7 Hz apart in pitch, 101 Hz in timbre. Every other speaking pair in the cut is cleanly separated (Guard 1 is 22.8 Hz off the tree, the scavenger 57.0 Hz). One defect, exactly where he said it was.

Cause: a casting gap, not a mis-wire. genomes/sapling/voices.yaml does give Guard 2 his own voice (am_echo) and synth_vo.py does clone from his own cb-refs/am_echo.wav — that chain is correct end to end. What never happened is the shaping that makes a clone sound like a different person: build_refs.py's VOICE_SHAPING gives a hand-written character passage and a semitone offset to am_puck, bm_george, bm_daniel and bf_isabella only. Both guards were cast after that 2026-07-25 pass and have no entry, so both were cloned from the same shared paragraph at neutral pitch — the convergence that file's own comment calls “the acoustic cause of the founder's 'voices are mixed up'”. Their refs land 1.1 Hz (Guard 1) and 8.4 Hz (Guard 2) from bm_fable's. Guard 1 drifted clear in synthesis by luck; Guard 2 did not.
