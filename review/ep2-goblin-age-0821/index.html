1. Your picture — the reference everything below is measured against

“dude, this is how the goblin should look”

That picture replaced the old adult design on every axis it shows — ears, eyes, height, skin, clothes. No tusk, no patchwork cloak; those belonged to the design it replaced. Everything made before 16:54 is the wrong character, kept as evidence and not used.

Seven beats were redrawn against it, then handed to the motion model. That is what the rest of this page is.

2. The seven heads next to yours

The one difference I can see without being asked: the eyes. Yours are narrow and half-lidded, with a small pale slit and a heavy brow. All seven of ours came back with big round anime eyes and a pale green iris. The ears are bigger and more elvish than yours too. Everything else — bald dome, green skin, mandarin collar, dark shorts, boots — matches. If the eyes are wrong, say so and it is a one-line change to the recipe, not a re-render of the design.

3. The seven beats: still, then the clip made from it

Left is the drawn plate. Right is the clip — 704×1280, 105 frames, 4.4 seconds, no sound. Tap to play. Verdicts are mine and you can overrule any of them.

BEAT 02 — THE SPRINTFAIL

He sprints in, skids, and dives behind the sapling's thin trunk. The most frightened he is in the episode.

The camera pulls back until he is a speck at the bottom right of a wide shot, and a big bare tree that is in no plate grows to fill the frame. No sprint, no skid, no dive. His face stays drawn the whole time — this is a staging failure, not a character one. (The small creature at his boots is a known fault in this plate, already logged.)

BEAT 03 — BAD COVERFAIL

He crouches behind a trunk that hides about one-sixth of him, and believes it is working.

A curved horn or topknot grows out of his bald head about two-thirds through and stays. The camera drifts back and he shrinks by half. There is no trunk anywhere in frame, so the joke — bad cover — never happens. The face itself never dissolves.

BEAT 04 — THE FOOTNOTEPASS

He leans out to look, and pulls straight back the moment he has looked. Your own restaging pick from yesterday.

The best clip in the set. There is a real lean out and a real pull back, and both land inside the cut — the motion two rounds of drawing failed to get. The frame holds still. What it is not is a peek: with no trunk in shot there is nothing to lean out from, so it reads as a stumble and recover.

BEAT 07 — CONFISCATEFAIL

Guard 1 points at him, decisive. He has just been identified.

The guard finally shows up — armoured, helmeted, an adult, which is what you ruled — and the camera pulls back to fit him in. Then the goblin walks out of frame, so the last second is a guard pointing at empty grass. The beat is a guard pointing at him, so it fails even though the hard part worked.

BEAT 08 — INSIDE HIMPASS

Guard 2 points at his belly. He is caught, and the thing he is caught with is already eaten.

Clean on everything I score: face drawn in every frame, clothes hold, frame locked, and the look-down lands early. The cost is a taste call and it is yours: from about halfway, over half the clip is the top of his bowed head, so his face is small for most of it.

BEAT 13 — THE SHADEPASS

His legs give out, he sits, and tips his head into the sapling's hand-sized patch of shade. “...Thanks for the shade.”

Face holds to the last frame, where the previous version of this beat lost it halfway. Frame locked, clothes hold. Two soft spots: the head-tip finishes just after the cut point, so in the episode it reads as begun rather than done; and his eyes close early, so the canon eye — the whole point of your picture — is only readable for the first two seconds.

BEAT 20 — EVIDENCEFAIL

He picks the fig up with both hands and looks from it to the sapling's thinnest branch. “...Did you just ANSWER me?” The episode's turn.

He never looks up, and the look up is the beat — the same failure as the take this was meant to replace. The pose barely changes at all, so it is close to a still with a runtime. His face is arguably the cleanest in the set. The fig is yellow-green; canon says purple, and that came in with the plate, not the motion.

4. Why the four failed, and what is already running

All four failures name something the drawing does not contain. Beats 02 and 03 say “the thin sapling trunk”, 20 says “the branch”, 07 says “the guard” — and none of those is in the still the clip starts from. In every one of the four the camera pulled back, and in beat 02 the model went ahead and built the tree. Handed a word for something it cannot see, this model rebuilds the shot to make room for it.

Beat 04 is the control: its plate is missing the trunk too, but its instructions never name it — and beat 04 is the one that held still. So it is naming the absent thing that moves the camera, not missing it. The fix is the picture, not the wording.

Fixed plates are rendering right now — no waiting on this page:

  • 02, 03, 20 — the sapling and the branch were painted into the plates themselves and redrawn in place (his figure untouched, measured). Beat 02's re-roll has already finished on the card, 03 is rendering as this page went up, 20 is next in the queue.
  • 07 — needs a two-figure plate with the guard already in it. The first attempt drew a good guard but lost the goblin's eyes and collar, so a corrected plate is queued ahead of the clip.
  • 04, 08, 13 — nothing queued. They are candidates, waiting on you.

Update, while this page was being written: 02, 03 and 20 came back on the fixed plates. The camera fault is gone — painting the sapling and the branch into the picture stopped the pull-back on 02 and 20, and all but stopped it on 03. None of the three does its action yet: he still does not sprint, does not take cover, does not look up. So the plate buys the framing and it does not buy the movement, which is the next thing to solve and does not need you. The clips above are the round these verdicts describe; the new ones go up when they are judged as a set.

Nothing here has gone into the episode. The shipping cut is untouched and stays that way until you say otherwise.

5. The one question

Does this read as your goblin?
