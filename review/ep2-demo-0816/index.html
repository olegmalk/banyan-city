The pick: full-s2 (arm full, seed 20260902)

It is the only take that performs all three scripted verbs, keeps the costume, and neither freezes nor empties the frame. He rises off the grass in a smooth eased arc across frames 40–64, fusses the cloak straight, turns, and walks out on a real alternating stride. That is stands, brushes off, and turns to go, in script order.

This is a steward pick with reasons, not a taste verdict — taste is yours (R4). It was made by opening consecutive frames and looking at them, not by ranking a metric. The runners-up are listed so you can veto, not so you have to compare.

Its named faults, because it has them

  • It judders. Hold period 3 — 96 frames carry about 32 distinct pictures, an effective 8 fps, not 24. This is the same defect the rest of the cut has.
  • The last frame is a motion smear as he strides out of frame. In the assembled cut it is one frame at the very end of the beat and the cut to beat 18 lands on top of it.
  • The light is wrong. The plate is midday blue; the script says afternoon. That is a grade note, inherited from the plate, and motion cannot fix it.

The runners-up (for veto only)

  • full-s1 — the biggest head rise in the whole set (39.7%), and he walks right past camera and out. Rejected because the frame then sits empty for the last half-second and the exit is a huge cloak wipe across the lens. Its chart-topping rise number is also partly him walking towards the camera rather than standing taller, which is exactly why the number was not allowed to decide.
  • turn-s2 — a strong ending, cloak swirling as he goes. Rejected because he sits nearly still for the first 2.3 seconds, more than half the beat, before anything happens.

All twelve, measured — and how they were measured

Head rise and lateral travel come from pipeline/b17_figure_track.py, which colour-segments the goblin above the horizon and then draws the mask back onto the frames so a human can see whether it landed on him. Doing that caught a real bug: a bare ys.min() was being set by sky speckle and had silently scored three complete stand-ups as "rise 0".

Three things were deliberately NOT used to rank these takes: depth (it is inverted — the clip with the most real body motion in this repository scores 0.290 while a clip with no human motion at all scores 0.516); the retired cadence metric (structurally blind — every odd hold period aliases to exactly 1.00x); and camera-scale estimates, which are unreliable on our clips. And the bar's other half — "his head crosses the frame midline" — is undecidable on this plate: the head starts 2.6 px from the midline, so the literal 4-of-12 crossing count carries no information. Lateral travel is quoted instead.

Head rise is in half-scale pixels and as a fraction of frame height; lateral travel is head-centroid range at half-scale (frame is 352 px wide at that scale). "Terminal freeze" is the number of trailing frames whose consecutive-pair correlation is exactly 1.0000 (hard) and whose mean absolute difference is under 0.05 grey levels (soft) — reported both ways because crf-18 is not lossless and leaves per-pixel differences up to 8 on a provably frozen tail. Every one of these numbers is a filter. The cold read decided.

What beat 17 does NOT prove

It does not upgrade any other beat. What it does overturn is a claim six lanes had been converging on — that this engine cannot move a figure's body at all. Hips leaving the ground cannot be faked by re-inking or by a camera push, the horizon stays fixed while the goblin rises, and it happened in 12 cells out of 12. So whatever is wrong with beats 06, 10 and 13, "the model cannot animate a person" is not it.
