### Batch=2 is the sweet spot; batch=4 is the cliff

Throughput per second of video: 65.2 s at batch 1 → **45.3 s at batch 2** (a real
31% gain) → 109.3 s at batch 4 (2.4x *worse* than batch 2). `peak_torch_gb` tells
you why: 17.9 → 19.7 → **23.2 of 25.7 GB**. At batch 4 the card is at 90% and
spilling. This matters for the crash below.

### LICENCE CAVEAT — this timing is NOT the publishable recipe

`bench-a14b.cmd` ran **"Lightning LoRAs at strength 1.0 on both experts"**. Those
are the adapters `farm-queue.yaml` refused on licence grounds: `lightx2v/Wan2.2-Lightning`
declares apache-2.0 in HF metadata and ships **no LICENSE file**, so the gate calls
it unknown and footage made with it is unpublishable. `no_lora: true` was a licence
decision, not a performance one.

Without the LoRAs the distilled 4-step schedule is gone and the recipe is 25 steps
at guidance 5.0. At the measured **136.38 s/step** for 704x1280 production:

> **25 steps x 136.38 s = 3410 s ≈ 57 minutes per 2.5 s beat**, plus 242 s load.

Against ti2v-5b's measured 188 s for the same beat at the same size, the
licence-clean AnimeGen path is **~18x slower** (1341 vs 75 s of compute per second
of video). Fifteen beats would be ~15 GPU-hours. That is the number the model
choice actually turns on, and it is now measured rather than guessed.

## The new bluescheck — NOT Norton, NOT host RAM

```
23:31:00  night-chain STARTED
23:31:00  [1/4] bake AnimeGen HIGH expert -> fp8   rc=0  (2.4 min)
23:33:25  [2/4] bake LOW expert -> fp8              rc=0  (2.4 min, 14.58GB from 28.58GB bf16)
23:35:49  [3/4] AnimeGen sample from baked fp8      rc=9  RAM WATCHDOG ABORT
23:38:26  [4/4] ti2v-5b two-mode batch curve        <- RUNNING WHEN THE BOX DIED
00:09:28  last write to bench-5b-modes.log
~00:09    BUGCHECK 0x00020001, bucket INTEL_IOMMU_TIMEOUT_IMAGE_GenuineIntel.sys
          dump: C:\Windows\Minidump\080526-14562-01.dmp
00:11:42  back up
```

**Different fault from 08-03 and 08-04.** Both of those were `0x3B` / `0xc0000005`
/ bucket `AV_nllNetHub!unknown_function` — Norton's network filter. This one is
`0x20001` / `INTEL_IOMMU_TIMEOUT` — an IOMMU (VT-d) DMA-remapping timeout, a
platform-level fault.

**Norton is exonerated for this one and vindicated for the others:** its bucket
appeared twice (08-03 11:33, 08-04 11:30) and has not appeared since removal.

What it was doing instead: step 4, the ti2v-5b batch curve, at `batch=4`,
704x1280. `bench-5b-modes.log` shows per-step time collapsing mid-run —
**179 s → 175 s → 383 s** — with `peak torch 23.5 GB` already recorded at batch 2
on a 25.7 GB card. Host RAM was *not* the constraint (37.9 / 68.1 GB, 30.2 free).
So: VRAM oversubscribed at batch 4 → WDDM pages GPU memory over PCIe → sustained
heavy DMA → IOMMU timeout.

Stated honestly: **correlation, not proof.** The dump has not been analysed. But it
is the same `batch=4` cliff the AnimeGen table independently shows, arriving from a
different model — which is what makes it worth acting on rather than filing.

Secondary suspect worth ruling out cheaply: BIOS **E17S3IMS.10B dated 2025-05-12**
on an MSI Vector 17 HX AI A2XWJG — 15 months old. IOMMU timeouts are a classic
firmware-fix class, and the RAM was just changed, which alters the DMA/memory
topology this fault lives in.

## What worked, and deserves saying

- **The RAM watchdog did its job.** Step 3 aborted deliberately at
  `phys free 0.6GB < 1.0GB AND commit 127.8/130.4GB > 97%`, logging *"This is the
  0xC0000005 signature; aborting deliberately so the box survives."* That is the
  08-04 failure mode caught and prevented rather than repeated.
- **The fp8 bake worked** — both experts, 14.58 GB from 28.58 GB bf16, rc=0.
- **But both baked experts still do not fit even 64 GB.** The abort came while
  loading the *second* expert with the first already resident. Load order and fp8
  together are still not enough for a simultaneous two-expert load here.

## Recommendations

1. **Cap batch at 2** for both models on this card. Batch 2 is the throughput
   optimum; batch 4 is slower *and* is the best current suspect for the bluescheck.
2. **Do not re-run the batch=4 legs** until the dump is read or the BIOS is updated.
   Rerunning is the one action that could turn a one-off into a pattern.
3. **Show the founder the AnimeGen clips** before any further AnimeGen spend — R4,
   and the whole point of the sample.
4. **Decide AnimeGen on the licence-clean number (~57 min/beat), not the 4-step
   one.** The measured 545 s is a LoRA figure and that footage cannot ship.
5. **BIOS update** is the cheap platform-level move, especially post-RAM-change.
6. The `worker-log.txt` append-only fix is still worth landing — it failed twice.
