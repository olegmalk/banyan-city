The lab · quality tiers

What "cheaper" costs: cheap settings don't just blur — they redraw

Measured 2026-07-30 · all 15 beats × 3 settings = 45 frames · one machine

Every beat of episode 1 (a beat is one shot) drawn three times on the same machine, from the same prompt, changing only how long the AI is allowed to work: 122 seconds a frame, 62 seconds, 16 seconds. Fewer steps — fewer refinement passes — is the cheap part.

The middle column is the real question: is the 60-second setting good enough to iterate with? The right column is the drafting floor. The left is what a frame costs once it is canon (canon = the version the show actually uses). The answer, visible in every block below, is that the cheap tiers don't degrade a frame — they invent a different one. Fine for testing a composition; useless as a stand-in for an approved shot.

Provenance

All 45 frames were rendered on 2026-07-30 on the second Mac — an M3 Pro, 36 GB (the machine the fleet benchmark lists at 125 s a frame; it appears there under its worker name). The 122 s figure on this page and the 125 s figure there are the same machine measured on different work — this page timed the episode's own beats at 40 steps, the benchmark timed one fixed beat twice. The gap is measurement noise, not a different computer.

For scale: the gaming laptop in the farm renders this page's left-hand column in 13.5 s a frame. A projection for an M4 Pro puts the middle column near 45 s — that one is a sourced estimate, not a measurement, and it is the only number on this page that isn't ours.
