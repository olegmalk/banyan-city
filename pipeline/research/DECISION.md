- **The licence-clean substitute for Turbo's step reduction is our own step sweep, and it is founder-reserved.** 14→10 steps is 188s→153s and 14→8 is 188s→136s (DERIVED-FROM-OURS); the sweep already rendered on 2026-08-03 (`steps-06/10/14/20`). STATE.md lists "whether 14 steps is the quality bar" as open and founder-reserved, and step reduction is a known motion-flattener, so this is Alex's and the founder's call, not mine — but it costs nothing and needs no new weights.
- **Separately, for the founder's actual complaint ("not made for anime style"), two licence-clean anime-native candidates surfaced that were not in my inputs: `aidealab/AnimeGen-I2V` (Apache-2.0 with a real LICENSE file, `commercial-use` tagged, 4 steps in its own example) and `Index-anisora` V3.2 (Apache-2.0, natively 8-step).** Both are **Wan 2.2 A14B-class, 14B active, ~14.3GB per expert across two experts** — 28.6GB of weights on a 24GB card that already peaks at 22.9GB with a 5B model. **Speed on either: no data. Nobody has published a time benchmark for either on any card**, and the expert-swap cost is the dominant unknown. Do not plan a schedule around them. They are a ONE-SAMPLE, founder-screened taste call (R4), not a speed recipe.
- **Card B (5070 Ti, 12GB): do not render 704x1280 clips on it.** Seconds-per-clip: **no data**. Card A peaks at 22.9GB on this shape and activations dominate, so quantised weights do not rescue it; and ComfyUI #11775 is a host-RAM leak measured on exactly an RTX 5070 12GB / Blackwell / Win11 / cu128 running Wan2.2 GGUF. Give it T1/T2 stills, VO, and 480x832 drafts. If it ever must render, the only 12GB-targeted option in the field is `Index-anisora` **V3.1**, which shipped a 12GB-compatible build (ModelScope) — untimed by anyone.

## 3. Why we are at "13.4s per forward pass" — ranked

**We are almost certainly not.** 13.4 is `188 / 14`. The 20-step run gives `240 / 20 = 12.0`. A per-forward-pass cost cannot depend on how many passes you make, so both figures are averages contaminated by a fixed addend. Solving the two: **8.67s marginal per step + 66.7s fixed per sample.**

Separately, the brief labels 13.4s as the forward pass at 6,240 tokens (480x832/61f) while 18,480 tokens (704x1280/84f) costs 8.67s marginal. I recomputed both token counts from our stated geometry (patch [1,2,2], VAE 16x spatial / 4x temporal): exactly 6,240 and 18,480, so the big shape is 2.96x the work. Nothing does 2.96x the work in 65% of the time. Either the 13.4 was back-derived from 188/14 and misattributed to the small shape, or the small shape is *entirely* overhead-bound. Both readings land on cause 1.

1. **We are overhead-bound. ~67s per sample — 36% of wall clock — is not denoising, and most of it is model load we already know how to remove.** Evidence, three independent lines: the two-point fit above; STATE.md's note that the never-used batch path is worth "~12 min per episode" over 15 beats (≈48s/beat, within 20s of the fit); and `video_task.py:817`, "Per-clip processes spent ~10 of every 11 minutes reloading 10GB from disk." **Cheapest experiment:** queue one task with `beats: "1,2"` instead of two single-beat tasks. Costs one render, needs no install, and the code path already exists. (A `time.perf_counter()` around the denoise loop vs the whole `__call__` would attribute the residual ~19s to VAE decode and text encode; also free.)
2. **We were paging, and offload is what stopped it.** Evidence, ours and controlled: cycle-015 measured 480x832 with no offload at 462s against 704x1280 with offload at 240s — higher resolution, twice as fast, resolution the only other variable and pointing the wrong way. Diagnosed as the 24.4/26GB residency, i.e. the renderer paging against itself. Corroborating external framing: NVIDIA describe driver 536.40's sysmem fallback as preventing crashes "by allowing applications to run at lower speeds" ([KB a_id 5490](https://nvidia.custhelp.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/5490)), and a user measures ~10x ([A1111 #14077](https://github.com/AUTOMATIC1111/stable-diffusion-webui/discussions/14077)). We are at 22.9/25.7GB now, so we are probably still paying a slice of this. **Cheapest experiment:** the per-application control-panel toggle on python.exe. Free, and it converts an invisible tax into a legible OOM.
3. **No optimised attention kernel and no torch.compile.** Evidence: two independent MEASURED stacks agree on 1.5-2x for the whole attention+compile+cache layer (InstaSD 1.94x on a 4090; Voltage Park attributes ~10% to SageAttention alone, ~50% to TeaCache). Prebuilt sm_120 Windows wheels exist and the author claims ~35% on our exact card. **Cheapest experiment:** install the prebuilt wheel in a separate venv, one clip, same seed. Black frames are a *known Wan+Sage bug* with a known fix (KJNodes backend `sageattn_qk_int8_pv_fp16_cuda`) — do not go debugging our pipeline.
4. **`enable_model_cpu_offload` has no prefetch.** Evidence: mmgp and kijai's block swap both do async H2D with prefetch on by default (`prefetch_blocks=1`); diffusers' model offload does not, so it pays a serialised per-step transfer. Upgrade path is group offloading with `use_stream=True` ([diffusers #10503](https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/pull/10503)). But note cause 2: offload is currently a 1.93x *win* for us, so this is a refinement, not a defect. **Cheapest experiment:** only after 1 and 2 — swap to group offload with `use_stream=True`, same beat, same seed.
5. **Runtime dtype may be fp32, not bf16.** Evidence: `tooling.md` flags that the transformer ships fp32 on disk and the runtime dtype was never verified. Counter-evidence, strong: fp32 would put us far worse than the ~1.2-1.4x we sit off the official 4090. Ranked last on likelihood, but **run it first** — it is one `print(pipe.transformer.dtype)`.

Ranked by evidence strength, because the research does not settle it: nobody has profiled a TI2V-5B forward pass on any Blackwell card in public.

## 5. Splitting one clip across the two laptops

**No.** Tensor-parallel needs an all-reduce per attention and per MLP block. One hidden-state tensor at 18,480 tokens x 3072 hidden in bf16 is 113.5MB; 30 layers x 2 collectives = **6.8GB per denoising step**, which at a realistic ~113MB/s gigabit is **~60s of network against 8.7s of compute** — roughly 7x slower than one card. Pipeline-parallel at a single layer boundary is only ~114MB/step (~1s), but it does not cut single-clip latency at all: Card B idles while Card A runs its half. This is arithmetic from our own stated geometry (DERIVED-FROM-OURS, not measured); **the research contains no data on multi-GPU or LAN-distributed Wan inference** — `tooling.md` never reached that section, and our gigabit LAN has never been measured. The right use of two cards is one beat per card in parallel, which is what 15 beats needs anyway.

## 6. What nobody has data on

- **Anime / 2D cel competence: no public benchmark exists.** VBench-I2V's prompt set is general photographic/cinematic landscape content — zero anime entries, no AnimateDiff-family entry at all. Our concern #3, and the founder's actual complaint ("not made for anime style"), is **unmeasured by anyone.**
- **9:16 vertical:** nothing, in any suite.
- **"Did it invent a second stem":** partial at best. VBench-I2V `Video-Image Subject Consistency` is DINO similarity (TI2V-5B scores 98.43% — high, and it still happened to us); VBench-2.0 `Instance Preservation` is the nearest concept but is scored **from a text prompt, not a conditioning image**, so it cannot catch content that was never in our approved still.
- **Any quantised or distilled build's effect on dynamic degree:** zero data. No GGUF, fp8, or Turbo entry on VBench-I2V. Nor any stopwatch or quality comparison for TI2V-5B-Turbo on any card — demos only.
- **The two anime-native candidates have no speed data of any kind and no VBench-I2V entry.** Neither `AnimeGen-I2V` nor `Index-anisora` V3.2 publishes a generation time on any GPU (I checked AnimeGen-I2V's card directly), and neither appears on any dynamic-degree leaderboard. Both are A14B-class two-expert models whose weights (~28.6GB) exceed Card A's VRAM, so their offload behaviour — the thing that would decide whether they are usable at all — is **entirely unmeasured**. This is the largest single gap standing between us and the founder's actual complaint.
- **RTX 5090 (laptop or desktop) Wan 2.2:** no credible public measurement. **torch.compile on Windows + sm_120:** unverified everywhere. **cu128 vs cu130 on Blackwell:** one SEO-suspect source claims cu128 underperforms; uncorroborated, treat cu130 as unverified.
- **Ours:** the runtime transformer dtype, the batch path with offload enabled, and the LAN, are all unverified by us.
- Caution on every leaderboard number above: VBench-I2V is self-submitted and self-sampled, its `Accessibility` column is broken (dates appear in the open/closed field), and at least one row (CogVideoX1.5-5B-I2V) is column-shifted.

One thing the benchmarks *do* settle, and it outranks any speed number here: **frozen frames is the field's dominant failure mode, and our incumbent is mid-pack.** TI2V-5B scores Dynamic Degree **52.85%** — about half its clips classify as not moving at all. LTX-2-I2V, already ruled out on licence, tops the board on subject consistency (99.38%) and motion smoothness (99.29%) at **DD 8.54%**; Animate-Anything is DD 2.68%. The best open-weight compromise on the board is **Magi-1 (DD 68.21%, VI-Subj 98.39%)**, the only open entry simultaneously top-4 on dynamism and top-quartile on faithfulness — but it is 24B-class, its VRAM footprint is **no data**, and it is not this decision.
