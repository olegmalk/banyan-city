# Guidance 2.0 -> 1.0 halves the hold: the first lever that has ever moved the period

Measurement pass, 2026-08-15/16. **One recipe change, one sample, one matched
control rendered in the same job.** Nothing is scaled off this. $0 — local card,
no provider, two clips of 238 s and 131 s on an otherwise idle 5090.

This fires lever **L4** of `pipeline/research/ltx23-motion-source.md`
(`dfa87c27`), and closes lever **L6** with no generation at all (§5).

Spec: `pipeline/jobs/ep2-b13-guidance-0815.yaml`. **No renderer code was
touched** — `--guidance` has been a flag on `ltx_i2v.py` since long before
tonight, and its own default is already 1.0. The job yaml is the entire diff.

---

## The answer

**Dropping classifier-free guidance from 2.0 to the value the official distilled
config ships with halves the hold period and adds 50% more distinct pictures.**
Every other lever fired on this beat — five wordings, three seeds, the init
plate's onset, the decode side end to end, STG at upstream's recommended
1.0/`[28]` — left `period 3 / 32 pictures / 8.0 fps` exactly where it was. This
one did not.

| | control, guidance **2.0** | guidance **1.0** |
|---|---|---|
| hold period | **3** | **2** |
| autocorrelation at that lag | 0.923 | 0.913 |
| trough/peak **depth** | **0.038** | **0.035** |
| distinct pictures in 97 frames | **32.0** | **48.0** |
| **effective fps** | **8.0** | **12.0** |
| terminal freeze (bit-identical frames) | none | none |
| last change above 0.05 MAD | frame 96 of 96 | frame 96 of 96 |
| mean full-res per-pair MAD | 3.76 | **8.91** |
| frame-mean luma swing over the clip | 2.9 of 255 | **81.4 of 255** |
| frames 0->96 registrable by scale+shift | yes, ncc 0.873 | **NO — not admissible** |
| render time | 238 s | **131 s (-45%)** |
| peak torch VRAM | 9.9 GB | 5.5 GB |

The comb is the finding, and it is a **different** comb in each arm.
Full-resolution per-pair mean absolute difference, frames 40-48:

```
control (2.0)   0.6  0.4 10.5 | 0.6  1.1  8.4 | 0.4  0.4 10.2      one loud pair every THREE
guidance 1.0    1.4 14.1  0.8 |10.8  0.6 13.4 | 0.5 20.5  0.6      one loud pair every TWO
```

**Depth barely moved (0.038 -> 0.035), and that is the point.** Both arms still
hold — 1.0 is not a clip that moves on every frame, it is a clip that holds every
*two* frames as deeply as the control holds every three. Reference depths:
b13-AFTER 0.029, STG arm 0.059, b06-DONE 0.181, b02-FIXED 0.431, a clean VAE
round trip 0.971. A metric is a filter; §4 is the cold read.

## The extra pictures are NOT exposure flicker — measured, because they look like it

Guidance 1.0 swings the whole frame's brightness by **81 of 255** across the clip
(the control swings 2.9). A global luminance swing moves every pixel and inflates
mean absolute difference exactly like real motion does, so it had to be ruled out
before the 48 could be believed. Each frame's mean luma was removed and the pairs
re-differenced:

| | raw mean pair MAD | DC-removed | period after DC removal | distinct |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| control 2.0 | 3.79 | 3.91 | 3 | 32.0 |
| guidance 1.0 | 8.87 | **9.92** | **2** | **48.0** |

Removing the exposure swing makes the difference series **larger**, not smaller —
the DC term was partially *cancelling* structural change, not creating it. So the
period-2 and the 48 distinct pictures are **structural**. That is a real
measurement of the thing the brief said no lever had moved.

## What the frames actually show, and why this is not yet a win

`pipeline/research/EVIDENCE-cfg-b13-0815.png`, three panels. I opened them.

- **A — whole frame, 9 frames across the clip.** The control row is nine copies
  of one picture. The 1.0 row is visibly different in every tile: brighter at
  f24, much darker from f60, with drifting light specks appearing in the last
  third that are in neither the prompt nor the control.
- **B — the figure, ONE fixed box (y260-900, x60-620) in BOTH rows**, so any
  size change on the sheet is real and not a per-row crop. The control is one
  picture from f0 to f96. The 1.0 arm redraws the figure continuously: the head
  grows and shifts, the face changes — eyes wider and the jaw longer at f32,
  narrower at f96 — the cast shadow behind him appears and leaves, and the arms
  fold across the knees differently at f80 than at f0.
- **C — nine consecutive frames, 40-48**, the same 200x200 window in both rows at
  2x, chosen by summed absolute difference rather than by eye, with the per-pair
  MAD printed on each tile so the grouping can be checked against the picture.
  The control advances in threes. The 1.0 arm advances in twos.

**Neither figure rocks.** The prompt's action clause is "He rocks slowly forward
and back", and across seven frames spanning the whole clip both arms hold the
same folded pose. What guidance 1.0 bought is **more pictures, not the scripted
action**: the extra pictures are identity churn, lighting swing and framing
drift. That is a real and useful finding about the hold — and it is not the beat.

### The background stops being the same background

This is the strongest caution, and it is a measurement rather than an impression.
`align_frames.py` takes an action-free band (top 30%: sky, hedge, hills) and
brute-forces scale and integer shift, refusing to quote a number it cannot stand
behind.

```
control 2.0   f0->f96   best scale 1.000 dx  +2 dy +12   ncc 0.873   admissible
guidance 1.0  f0->f48   best scale 1.000 dx  +0 dy  +9   ncc 0.687   admissible
guidance 1.0  f0->f96   NOT ADMISSIBLE — best ncc 0.263 even with the search
                        widened to scale 0.80..1.40 and +-60 px
guidance 1.0  f48->f96  NOT ADMISSIBLE — best ncc 0.118
```

The control's background is the same background at f96, moved 12 px. The 1.0
arm's background at f96 **cannot be brought into register with f0 by any zoom or
pan in a wide search**. That is not a camera move; it is content drift — the
thing `cut to another shot, scene change, different location` was in the negative
to prevent.

**And at guidance 1.0 the negative is dead.** `do_classifier_free_guidance` is
`guidance_scale > 1.0`, so at 1.0 there is no uncond pass and the negative
embeddings are never read — `ltx_i2v.py` documents this at the `common = dict(...)`
call. That is inherent to the flag, not a second variable smuggled in: you cannot
have CFG 1.0 and a live negative. It is why the 2.0 was presumably chosen in the
first place, and it is exactly what the drift looks like.

### The motion front-loads and fades

Full-resolution per-pair MAD by twelfths, DC removed:

```
control 2.0   4.66  3.00  3.88  4.76  3.28  4.12  3.87  2.91  4.41  4.55  2.72  4.83
guidance 1.0  9.87 14.72 17.75 12.63 13.31 10.60  9.14  9.72  7.04  6.80  3.57  2.98
```

The 1.0 arm peaks in the first quarter and decays monotonically, **ending below
the control**. Neither arm has the frame-62 wall — zero bit-identical pairs in
either, last change at frame 96 of 96 in both, so the two bark clips' literal
freeze is still a separate phenomenon and this pass says nothing about it. But
the 1.0 arm has a soft version of the same shape: it spends its motion early and
is quieter than the control by frame 88.

## Lever L6 (the 200-word prompt) is closed with NO generation, and the premise was wrong

The brief carried it as "the LTX-2 README says keep prompts within 200 words; we
run ~500 — a documented divergence." Measured across the repo rather than
assumed:

- **Beat 13's control prompt is 37 words / 58 Gemma tokens** of a 1024 limit,
  measured on the real tokenizer (`Lightricks/gemma-3-12b-it-qat-q4_0-unquantized`)
  on the box before firing. Its negative is 25 words / 38 tokens. **The hold —
  period 3, 32 pictures, 8.0 fps — was measured on a prompt already well inside
  the documented ceiling.** The 200-word divergence cannot be its cause.
- **No beat-13 LTX motion render has ever used a >200-word prompt.** Across all
  31 beat-13 specs that invoke `ltx_i2v.py`, the maximum ever is **216 words**
  (`ep2-b13-unfreeze-0814`); the median of the current wording arms is 37-61.
- The ~500-word prompts are real but belong to **other beats** — 558 words at
  `ep2-b05-stand-0814`, 487 at `ep2-b05-final-0814`, 444 at `ep2-b09-scene-0814r`,
  435 at `ep2-b06-read-0814`, 432 at `ep2-b21-poses-0814` (whose payload key is
  misleadingly named `b13-motion-prompt.txt` while the spec reads `beat: 21`).
  All are LTX/Gemma prompts, not still-generator prompts — 479 of 480 prompt
  payloads in the repo are consumed by `ltx_i2v.py --stage encode`.
- **They are also all dated 0811-0814. None is dated 0815.** Of the last 15 LTX
  motion jobs, the median prompt is **59 words** (range 37-72). The long-prompt
  regime is already behind us.
- The 684-token figure in `dfa87c27` §3.3 comes from `pipeline/prompt_budget.py`,
  which sources it as "871 prompt files **on the box**, max 684" — files on the
  Windows render box, not committed specs. The largest committed prompt is 558
  words.

So L6 is not a lever that was left untested; it is a lever whose premise does not
hold on the beat where the hold is measured. **Retired.** If someone wants to ask
the 200-word question it has to be asked on beat 05 or 21, against a control
rendered from the same long prompt, and it would be a question about *those*
clips, not about this hold.

## How the two arms were kept matched, and why the control proves the harness

One crop from an asserted plate sha, **one encode feeding both arms** (so
"identical text conditioning" is the same `.pt` read twice rather than an
assumption about Gemma's determinism), one seed 20260815, both renders in one
job. The two render steps are generated from a single argv function whose only
recipe parameter is guidance; the generator asserts that **the sole recipe-flag
difference is `--guidance`** and that every other difference sits after `--jobs`,
`--task`, `--bench-jsonl` or `--bench-label`. It further asserts that our
control's argv **is the STG lane's control argv** modulo job-name plumbing, and
the prompt, negative, plate, plate sha and crop script are read out of
`pipeline/jobs/ep2-b13-stg-0815.yaml` rather than retyped.

The empirical half is the strongest evidence in this document:

```
42043851da4b246cfcd4e858cda828da992d0d48f15ef10d81d3a99c1ceb445a  13-the-shade-CFG-2p0-control.mp4
210932b760f70ef34b97f41a28d1905ef5358e5f6982110c3f2440884b8b5199  b13-init-704x1280.png
```

Both are **byte-for-byte** the shas the STG lane published, which were themselves
byte-for-byte the clip rendered before the STG code existed. **Three independent
jobs, on three different nights, from an asserted source plate through a fresh
crop, a fresh encode and a fresh denoise, produce the identical file.** The
comparison in this document rests on that, not on an assumption.

Recipe, unchanged and not under test: 704x1280, 97 frames, 24 fps, two-stage
8@352x640 + 3@704x1280, distilled sigmas, image-crf 33, sequential offload, STG
unpassed on both arms. Sidecars carry `cost_usd: 0`, `mode: production` and no
`approved:` key — the same shape the STG arm's sidecar has.

**A provenance defect noticed in passing, not fixed here.** `ltx_i2v.py` appends
`[unused: guidance 1.0 on the distilled path runs no uncond pass, so this changed
no pixel]` to the sidecar's negative **unconditionally**, without checking
`a.guidance`. On the 2.0 control that caption is false — CFG > 1, the negative
was live. It affects the sidecar text only, never a pixel. Flagged rather than
patched: this lane's variable is guidance, `ltx_i2v.py` has another lane's commit
on it tonight, and a one-line edit there would break the "no renderer code
touched" property this sample rests on.

## What this closes, what it opens, and what must not happen next

**Closed.** "No lever moves the period." One does. Guidance 2.0 -> 1.0, at the
value upstream's own distilled config ships, takes beat 13 from 32 distinct
pictures to 48 and from 8.0 to 12.0 effective fps, structurally, at 45% less
render time. Both primary sources predicted the direction — Lightricks' own
multimodal-guidance doc ("higher `cfg_scale` = ... potentially **less natural
motion**") and the STG paper's abstract ("CFG ... **reduce diversity and
motion**") — and the measurement agrees with them.

**Not closed, and deliberately not touched by one sample.** Everything else.
In particular:

- **This is not a recipe change and must not become one.** The 1.0 arm loses its
  background, swings 81/255 in exposure, redraws the face, and still does not
  perform the action. Shipping it across fifteen beats is precisely the 2026-08-03
  failure — a recipe picked on the steward's own metric, rendered wide, and
  returned as "literally just frozen frames". **32 -> 48 pictures of the wrong
  thing is not a better beat.**
- **Guidance between 1.0 and 2.0 is untested.** 1.5 would keep the negative
  alive (CFG > 1) while spending less of the motion the doc says CFG costs. That
  is the obvious next one-variable sample and this pass does not license it — it
  only shows the endpoint moves.
- **The negative's role is now a live question of its own.** At 2.0 the negative
  is active and the clip is stable and frozen; at 1.0 it is dead and the clip is
  unstable and moving. Which of those two effects is doing the work — the CFG
  strength or the negative's content — is not separable from this sample. A
  guidance-2.0 arm with an **empty** negative would separate them in one clip.
- **Whether any of this transfers off beat 13 is unknown.** One beat, one plate,
  one seed, one wording.
